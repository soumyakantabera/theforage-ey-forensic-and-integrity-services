## About the Program

The **EY Forensic & Integrity Services (FIS) Virtual Experience Program**, hosted on [The Forage](https://www.theforage.com/), is a self-paced simulation designed to replicate the day-to-day work of forensic investigators at Ernst & Young. Participants step into the role of a junior forensic analyst and are tasked with handling a realistic fraud investigation from initial complaint intake through to cross-border evidence gathering and internal reporting.

The program develops core competencies in:

- **Forensic Accounting** – identifying financial anomalies, over-invoicing, and fraudulent capitalisation.
- **Anti-Bribery & Corruption (ABC)** – applying international compliance frameworks (UK Bribery Act, FCPA) to real-world scenarios.
- **Whistleblower Management** – professionally deconstructing anonymous tip-offs into structured, actionable investigative steps.
- **Document Examination** – forensically reviewing procurement records for timing, pricing, and recording irregularities.
- **Cross-Jurisdictional Collaboration** – communicating with and briefing international forensic teams to coordinate evidence collection across borders.

## Case Background

The investigation centres on **ABC Group**, a multinational corporation headquartered in the United Kingdom, and its wholly owned Indian subsidiary, **DEF Private Limited**. ABC Group had engaged a Chinese-based equipment supplier, **XYZ LLC**, for a significant capital expenditure project at the DEF facility.

Two independent whistleblower complaints — received from a UK-based supplier and a Peru-based supplier — alleged serious procurement irregularities at DEF Private Limited. Both complaints pointed towards the **Head of Procurement** as the key individual suspected of:

- Awarding contracts to XYZ LLC without following a proper competitive tendering process.
- Receiving personal financial benefits (kickbacks) in return for directing business to XYZ LLC.
- Manipulating documentation to disguise the irregularities from internal audit and senior management.

The forensic team at EY was engaged to:
1. Review and deconstruct the whistleblower allegations.
2. Conduct a wealth analysis on the suspected individual.
3. Forensically review procurement documentation for evidence of fraud.
4. Coordinate with EY China to gather evidence directly from XYZ LLC's records.

## Task 1 – Understanding Fraud Investigation & the Fraud Triangle

### Background

Before diving into the case, it is essential for every forensic analyst to have a firm command of the core terminology used in fraud investigation and anti-bribery compliance work. The **Fraud Triangle**, developed by criminologist Donald Cressey, is the cornerstone framework of forensic investigation. It posits that fraud occurs when three elements converge:

- **Pressure (Motivation)** – The individual faces financial or personal pressure (e.g., debt, lifestyle expectations).
- **Opportunity** – Weak controls or a position of trust allows the individual to commit fraud without immediate detection.
- **Rationalization** – The individual convinces themselves that their actions are justifiable.

### Objective

Complete an interactive crossword puzzle designed to reinforce 12 key forensic and anti-bribery concepts that are central to the EY FIS methodology, including:

| Concept | Relevance to the Case |
|---|---|
| **Fraud** | The overarching category of misconduct under investigation |
| **Bribery** | Alleged kickbacks from XYZ LLC to the Head of Procurement |
| **Corruption** | Systemic abuse of entrusted authority for private gain |
| **Compliance** | The internal control framework that was bypassed |
| **Integrity** | The ethical standard expected of all ABC Group employees |
| **Due Diligence** | Required prior to onboarding XYZ LLC as a supplier |
| **Investigation** | The structured process EY FIS is conducting |
| **Red Flag** | Observable indicator of potential misconduct |
| **Whistleblower** | The anonymous individuals who raised the initial complaints |
| **Procurement** | The function at the centre of the alleged irregularities |
| **Kickback** | Suspected form of payment to the employee from the supplier |
| **Collusion** | Suspected coordination between the employee and XYZ LLC |

## Task 2 – Deconstruction of Whistleblower Complaints

### Background

Whistleblower complaints are often the first — and sometimes the only — entry point into an otherwise concealed fraud scheme. However, anonymous complaints are typically unstructured, emotionally charged, and may contain a mix of factual observations, hearsay, and speculation. A key skill in forensic investigation is the ability to professionally deconstruct such complaints into discrete, verifiable issues that can inform a structured investigation plan.

In this case, two separate complaints were received:

- **Complaint 1 (UK Supplier)** – Alleged that DEF Private Limited's Head of Procurement had a pre-existing personal relationship with the owner of XYZ LLC, and that contracts were awarded without a proper bidding process, bypassing multiple qualified UK-based suppliers.
- **Complaint 2 (Peru Supplier)** – Alleged that the Head of Procurement had demanded a percentage commission ("facilitation fee") in exchange for awarding a supply contract — a classic kickback arrangement — and that this was communicated covertly.

### Objective

Consolidate both complaints into a single, professionally structured **Complaint Deconstruction Note** that:

1. Summarises each allegation clearly and objectively.
2. Identifies the **key issues** raised (contract irregularities, conflict of interest, bribery/kickbacks).
3. Maps each allegation to the relevant **investigative step** required to confirm or refute it.
4. Highlights any **corroborating elements** between the two independent complaints.
5. Notes any **gaps or limitations** in the information provided.

### Key Issues Identified

| Issue | Allegation Source | Investigative Step |
|---|---|---|
| Conflict of interest / undisclosed personal relationship | UK Supplier | Review employee declarations; interview key personnel |
| Bypassing competitive tender process | UK Supplier | Obtain and review full procurement records for XYZ LLC contracts |
| Solicitation of kickbacks / facilitation fees | Peru Supplier | Review financial records of Head of Procurement; examine supplier payment terms |
| Pattern of contract favouritism | Both Suppliers | Analyse all contracts awarded to XYZ LLC vs. competing bids |

### Deliverable

A completed **Complaint Deconstruction Note** (DOCX format) that methodically breaks down each allegation, maps issues to investigative procedures, and provides a foundation for the wider investigation.

## Task 3 – Analysing Financial Information Related to Suspected Fraud

### Background

One of the most powerful indicators of occupational fraud is a visible disconnect between an individual's **known legitimate income** and their **observed lifestyle and wealth**. When an employee is receiving illicit payments — such as kickbacks — this often manifests in unexplained asset acquisitions, lavish spending, and an overall standard of living inconsistent with their salary.

In forensic investigations, this is formalised through a **Wealth Analysis** (also known as a **Lifestyle Review**). The goal is not to jump to conclusions, but to systematically document observable red flags that, taken together, build a picture warranting further investigation and — where necessary — formal asset tracing.

The **Head of Procurement** at DEF Private Limited was identified as the primary suspect based on the whistleblower allegations in Task 2. A wealth analysis diagram was provided, showing the suspect's disclosed income sources, known expenditures, and recently acquired assets.

### Objective

Analyse the wealth diagram and identify all financial red flags using the structured red-flag analysis template. For each red flag, document:

- The **nature of the anomaly** (e.g., unexplained asset, lifestyle inconsistency).
- The **financial value or materiality** where estimable.
- The **recommended investigative next step** (e.g., obtain bank statements, conduct asset search).

### Red Flags Identified

| Red Flag | Description | Recommended Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Luxury vehicle acquisition** | Purchase of a high-value vehicle not consistent with known salary | Obtain bank/loan records; trace source of funds |
| **Property investment** | Evidence of real estate purchase(s) in excess of affordable range | Cross-reference with land registry; request mortgage documents |
| **Overseas travel expenditure** | Frequent international travel inconsistent with salary level | Review expense claims and personal travel bookings |
| **Private school fees** | Payment of school tuition fees beyond normal salary affordability | Request personal financial statements |
| **Lifestyle spending pattern** | Regular high-value restaurant, retail, and entertainment expenditure | Obtain personal bank account statements |
| **Sudden wealth increase** | Rapid accumulation of assets following the contract award to XYZ LLC | Timeline analysis: map asset acquisition against contract award dates |

### Connection to the Fraud Triangle

The wealth analysis provides direct evidence of the **Pressure** and **Opportunity** legs of the Fraud Triangle. The suspect's financial lifestyle suggests they had significant personal financial aspirations (pressure) and, in their role as Head of Procurement, possessed the authority and access to facilitate kickback payments (opportunity).

### Deliverable

A completed **Employee Wealth Red-Flag Analysis Template** (DOCX format) with all identified anomalies documented, ranked by risk, and mapped to follow-up investigative actions.

## Task 4 – Cross-Border Collaboration with International EY FIS Teams

### Background

As the investigation progressed, it became clear that the key evidence resided not only in DEF Private Limited's records in India, but also within the financial and operational records of **XYZ LLC** in China. Cross-border investigations introduce significant additional complexity, including jurisdictional differences in data access rights, language barriers, document authenticity verification, and the need to coordinate evidence gathering across time zones and legal frameworks.

EY's global network of Forensic & Integrity Services teams provides a distinct advantage in such scenarios. The EY India team engaged the **EY China team** to assist in obtaining and reviewing XYZ LLC's records — a standard practice in multinational forensic engagements.

This task was structured in three parts.

---

### Part 1 – Forensic Document Analysis

**Documents Reviewed:**

| Document | Issuer / Source |
|---|---|
| Sample Purchase Order (PO) | DEF Private Limited → XYZ LLC |
| Invoice | XYZ LLC → DEF Private Limited |
| Credit Note | XYZ LLC → DEF Private Limited |
| Fixed Asset Register (FAR) | DEF Private Limited internal records |

**Critical Discrepancies Identified:**

1. **Invoice Pre-dates the Purchase Order**  
   The invoice from XYZ LLC bears a date that is *earlier* than the corresponding Purchase Order issued by DEF Private Limited. This is a serious red flag — it indicates the goods or services were invoiced before they were formally ordered, which is anomalous in legitimate procurement and strongly suggestive of document manipulation or collusion. In a standard procurement cycle, the PO must be raised first, triggering the supplier to deliver and then invoice.

2. **Price and Currency Mismatches**  
   The unit prices stated on the Invoice differ from those agreed in the Purchase Order. Additionally, the currency denomination on the Invoice is inconsistent with that specified in the PO, creating a discrepancy that would inflate the final payment amount — a hallmark of over-invoicing fraud.

3. **Partial Credit Note Masking Discrepancy**  
   A credit note was subsequently issued by XYZ LLC, but it covers only a fraction of the price discrepancy rather than the full amount. This partial correction appears calculated to reduce visibility of the overcharge while still allowing the majority of the excess payment to pass through undetected.

4. **Premature Capitalisation in the Fixed Asset Register**  
   The relevant assets were recorded in DEF's Fixed Asset Register and depreciation was commenced *before* physical delivery and acceptance of the goods could have reasonably taken place. This premature capitalisation inflates asset values and may have been used to justify the inflated invoice amounts to internal approvers.

**Deliverable:** Completed Document Analysis Report (DOCX) summarising all four documents, documenting each discrepancy with reference to the specific document and line item.

## Major Findings

Across the four tasks, the following significant findings emerged:

### 1. Documentary Evidence of Over-Invoicing
The invoice from XYZ LLC was found to exceed the agreed purchase order value due to unit price discrepancies and a currency inconsistency. The partial credit note recovered only a fraction of the overcharge, allowing the majority of the surplus payment to flow through. This is a textbook over-invoicing scheme.

### 2. Invoice Timing Anomaly — Strong Indicator of Collusion
The XYZ LLC invoice was dated *prior* to the corresponding DEF Private Limited Purchase Order. This sequencing is not commercially or operationally possible in a legitimate transaction and strongly suggests that documentation was fabricated or backdated, pointing to active collusion between the supplier and the DEF procurement function.

### 3. Premature Asset Capitalisation
Assets were entered into DEF's Fixed Asset Register and commenced depreciation before delivery could have occurred. This accounting irregularity served to validate inflated invoice amounts to internal stakeholders and auditors, potentially delaying detection of the fraud.

### 4. Corroborated Kickback Allegations
Two independent whistleblowers — from different countries, different industries, and with no apparent connection to each other — both independently alleged that the Head of Procurement was receiving personal financial benefits from XYZ LLC. This independent corroboration significantly strengthens the credibility of the allegations.

### 5. Living-Beyond-Means Evidence
The wealth analysis identified multiple lifestyle indicators — property, vehicle, travel, and education expenditure — that are inconsistent with the suspect's legitimate salary. The timing of several asset acquisitions aligns closely with the period of the XYZ LLC contract, suggesting the kickback payments began shortly after the contract was awarded.

### 6. End-to-End Evidentiary Linkage
There is a clear and coherent evidentiary thread connecting:  
**Whistleblower complaints (Task 2)** → **Suspect lifestyle anomalies (Task 3)** → **Transactional and documentary fraud evidence (Task 4)**  
This convergence of independent evidence streams substantially increases the strength of the overall case.

## Conclusion

This virtual experience program offered a rigorous and realistic immersion into the full lifecycle of a forensic investigation — from the receipt of an anonymous tip-off through to cross-border evidence gathering and professional team briefing. Each task built logically upon the previous one, mirroring how real EY Forensic & Integrity Services engagements are structured and executed.

### Key Learnings

**1. Complaint Handling as an Investigative Foundation**  
The disciplined deconstruction of whistleblower complaints is not merely an administrative task — it is the foundation upon which the entire investigation is built. A well-structured Complaint Deconstruction Note ensures that no allegation is overlooked, that investigative resources are correctly prioritised, and that the case can withstand scrutiny if it proceeds to litigation or regulatory referral.

**2. Financial Red Flags as Corroborating Evidence**  
Wealth analysis and lifestyle reviews are among the most powerful — and underutilised — investigative tools available to forensic practitioners. While financial red flags alone rarely constitute proof of fraud, they serve as critical corroborating evidence that, combined with documentary and testimonial evidence, can significantly strengthen a case. The linkage between the suspect's lifestyle anomalies and the timing of the contract award is a compelling example of this dynamic.

**3. Document Examination as the Evidentiary Core**  
The forensic review of the Purchase Order, Invoice, Credit Note, and Fixed Asset Register demonstrated how a small set of documents, examined in the right sequence and with the right level of scepticism, can yield powerful evidence of fraud. The invoice-predating-PO finding alone, in a real investigation, would be sufficient to justify a full forensic audit and potential referral to law enforcement.

**4. Cross-Border Collaboration as a Strategic Capability**  
Modern procurement fraud rarely stays within a single jurisdiction. The ability to mobilise international forensic teams — and to brief them effectively — is a core capability of the EY FIS network. The briefing materials prepared for the EY China team demonstrate how complex investigations must be communicated clearly, professionally, and comprehensively to enable seamless cross-border collaboration.

**5. The Fraud Triangle as an Analytical Lens**  
Throughout the investigation, the Fraud Triangle proved to be a consistently useful analytical framework:
- **Pressure**: The suspect's lifestyle aspirations created financial pressure beyond their legitimate means.
- **Opportunity**: The Head of Procurement role gave the suspect unchecked authority over supplier selection and contract approval.
- **Rationalization**: The use of a partial credit note and manipulated documentation suggests an active effort to obscure the misconduct — a typical rationalisation behaviour.

### Overall Assessment

The evidence gathered across all four tasks — corroborating whistleblower complaints, lifestyle red flags, document discrepancies, and the structural opportunity afforded by the procurement role — collectively builds a compelling and internally consistent picture of a kickback scheme. In a live engagement, the next steps would include:

- Formal interviews with the Head of Procurement and key XYZ LLC personnel.
- Forensic analysis of XYZ LLC's bank records (via EY China) to trace cash flows back to the suspect.
- Review of all other contracts awarded to XYZ LLC for similar irregularities.
- Referral to ABC Group's legal counsel to consider disciplinary action, civil recovery, and/or law enforcement referral.

This experience reinforced that effective forensic investigation requires not only analytical rigour and technical expertise, but also clear professional communication, meticulous documentation, and the ability to collaborate across disciplines, cultures, and borders.
