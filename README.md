# The Forage - EY Forensic & Integrity Services

## Introduction

This repository contains the complete deliverables from the **EY Forensic & Integrity Services Virtual Experience Program** hosted on The Forage platform. The program provides hands-on experience in forensic accounting, fraud investigation, and cross-border compliance work.

The simulation follows a realistic investigation into procurement irregularities and potential bribery at **ABC Group** and its Indian subsidiary **DEF Private Limited**, involving a Chinese supplier **XYZ LLC**. The case draws on real-world forensic techniques including whistleblower complaint deconstruction, financial red-flag analysis, document examination, and international team collaboration.

## Tasks Overview

### Task 1: Understanding Fraud Investigation and the Fraud Triangle
- **Objective**: Complete a crossword puzzle reinforcing key forensic and anti-bribery terminology (Fraud, Bribery, Corruption, Compliance, Integrity, Due Diligence, Investigation, Red Flag, etc.).
- **Deliverable**: Fully completed crossword grid with all 12 clues solved.

### Task 2: Deconstruction of Whistleblower Complaints
- **Objective**: Consolidate anonymous whistleblower complaints from UK and Peru suppliers into a single professional **Complaint Deconstruction Note**.
- **Deliverable**: Structured note breaking down allegations (contract favoritism, kickbacks, bidding irregularities) with corresponding investigative steps.

### Task 3: Analysing Financial Information Related to Suspected Fraud
- **Objective**: Identify "red flags" in an employee's (Head of Procurement) financial profile and lifestyle using a wealth analysis diagram.
- **Deliverable**: Completed red-flag analysis template documenting living-beyond-means indicators, sudden asset acquisitions, and recommended next steps.

### Task 4: Cross-Border Collaboration with International EY Forensic & Integrity Services Teams
**Part 1 – Document Analysis**  
Review of four key documents (Purchase Order, Invoice, Credit Note, Fixed Asset Register) revealing invoice predating PO, price/currency mismatches, partial credit note, and premature capitalization.

**Part 2 – Professional Communication**  
Drafted email to EY China team requesting collaboration, summarizing findings, and proposing a joint investigation plan.

**Part 3 – Briefing Materials**  
Comprehensive internal briefing pack for the Thursday meeting with EY China, including organizational mapping, evidence checklist, variance analysis procedures, and timeline.

**Major Findings Across the Case**:
- Clear evidence of **over-invoicing** by XYZ LLC (China) to DEF Pvt Ltd (India).
- Invoice issued **before** the Purchase Order — strong indicator of collusion.
- Partial credit note used to mask discrepancies while assets were fully capitalized.
- Multiple red flags consistent with **kickback scheme** benefiting the Head of Procurement.
- Strong linkage between whistleblower allegations (Task 2), employee lifestyle (Task 3), and transactional anomalies (Task 4).

## Conclusions

This virtual experience provided practical exposure to the full forensic investigation lifecycle:

1. **Complaint Handling** — Systematic deconstruction of whistleblower reports into actionable issues.
2. **Financial Analysis** — Identification of lifestyle and wealth red flags that often accompany occupational fraud.
3. **Document Examination** — Forensic review of procurement documents revealing timing, pricing, and recording anomalies.
4. **Cross-Border Collaboration** — Effective briefing and coordination with international teams (EY China) to access supplier records and verify cash flows.

The case demonstrates how the **Fraud Triangle** (Pressure, Opportunity, Rationalization) manifests in real procurement fraud scenarios and how EY Forensic & Integrity Services applies structured methodologies to uncover and evidence such schemes.

All deliverables are ready for submission or further review. The investigation highlights the importance of timely document analysis, cross-jurisdictional cooperation, and maintaining professional skepticism when red flags appear in both employee behavior and transactional records.
